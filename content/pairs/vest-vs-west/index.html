Vest vs West: Learn to Hear the Difference

Many English learners find vest and west difficult to distinguish. Only the first sound changes: vest begins with /v/ and west begins with /w/.

Why Do “Vest” and “West” Sound the Same?

vest /vɛst/

west /wɛst/

The vowel and final sounds are the same. This contrast can be difficult if your first language does not separate /v/ and /w/.

Vest vs West: The Sound Difference

WordIPAFirst sound
vest/vɛst//v/
west/wɛst//w/

Vest

Vest begins with /v/. To make /v/:

  • Place your upper teeth lightly against your lower lip.
  • Let air pass through.
  • Use your voice.

You should feel vibration. Listen: vest

West

West begins with /w/. To make /w/:

  • Round your lips.
  • Do not touch your teeth to your lower lip.
  • Move quickly into the vowel.

Listen: west

Can You Hear the Difference?

Listen: vest → /vɛst/; west → /wɛst/

Focus on the first sound:

  • Friction and lip-to-teeth contact → vest
  • Rounded lips and a smooth glide → west

The rest of each word is identical.

Why Learners Confuse V and W

Some languages do not use /v/ and /w/ as separate sound categories. Your brain may therefore hear both as one familiar sound. This can affect words such as vest / west, vine / wine, and veil / whale. Focused listening helps your brain notice which sound begins the word.

How to Practice Vest vs West

  1. Listen to the beginning of the word.
  2. Decide whether you heard /v/ or /w/.
  3. Choose vest or west.
  4. Check your answer.
  5. Repeat with related pairs.

Listen first. Once the distinction is clearer, practice producing it.

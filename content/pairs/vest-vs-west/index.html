Why Do “Vest” and “West” Sound the Same?

Many English learners find vest and west difficult to distinguish. The words are very similar: vest /vɛst/ and west /wɛst/. The vowel and ending sounds are identical.

Only the first sound changes: vest begins with /v/ and west begins with /w/. That small consonant difference changes the word. Minimal-pair practice helps your ear recognize the difference between these two sounds.

Vest vs West: The Sound Difference

WordIPAFirst sound
vest/vɛst//v/
west/wɛst//w/

The difference is at the beginning of the word: _est. The ending stays the same. Only the first sound changes.

Vest

Vest begins with the /v/ sound. To make /v/:

  • Place your upper teeth lightly against your lower lip.
  • Let air pass through the small opening.
  • Use your voice.

You should feel vibration in your lip and throat. Listen: vest

West

West begins with the /w/ sound. To make /w/:

  • Round your lips.
  • Keep your teeth away from your lower lip.
  • Glide quickly into the vowel.

The sound starts with a smooth movement rather than friction. Listen: west

Can You Hear the Difference?

Listen carefully: vest → /vɛst/; west → /wɛst/. Focus only on the first sound.

  1. Listen: vest or west?
  2. Choose the word you hear.
  3. Check your answer.
  4. Repeat.

The rest of the word is identical. The first sound is the only clue. With focused listening practice, your brain becomes better at separating the two sounds.

Why Learners Confuse V and W

Some languages do not use /v/ and /w/ as separate sound categories. Your brain may therefore hear both as one familiar sound. This can affect words such as vest / west, vine / wine, and veil / whale. Focused listening helps your brain notice which sound begins the word.

Feel vs Fill: Learn to Hear the Difference

Why Do “Feel” and “Fill” Sound the Same?

Many English learners find feel and fill difficult to distinguish. The words are very similar: feel /fiːl/ and fill /fɪl/. The beginning and ending sounds are identical. Only the vowel changes.

That small vowel difference changes the meaning: feel = to experience a sensation, emotion, or physical impression; fill = to make something full. Minimal-pair practice helps your ear recognize the difference between these two sounds.

Feel

Feel uses /iː/. You hear this vowel in:

  • beat
  • see
  • sheep

It is typically longer and tenser. Listen: feel

Fill

Fill uses /ɪ/. You hear this vowel in:

  • bit
  • sit
  • ship

It is shorter and more relaxed. Listen: fill

Can You Hear the Difference?

Listen carefully: feel → /fiːl/; fill → /fɪl/. Focus on the vowel before the final l.

  1. Listen: feel or fill?
  2. Choose the word you hear.
  3. Check your answer.
  4. Repeat.

The final l is identical in both words, so the vowel is the only clue. The difference may seem small at first. With focused listening practice, your brain becomes better at separating the two sounds.

Why Learners Confuse Feel and Fill

If your first language does not treat /iː/ and /ɪ/ as separate sounds, your brain may place both into one familiar category. You may know the words when reading but still miss the distinction when listening. Reading gives you spelling clues. Conversation does not. Ear training helps your brain recognize the sounds without relying on the written word.
